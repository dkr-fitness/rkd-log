**Basis:** Kaiser OT eval 7/14/26 + 8/11/26 follow-up (Bhupinder Kaur) + 8/12/26 integration session
**Purpose:** Content source for future in-app glossary feature. Cues written in hardstyle strength-training voice — tension, bracing, structural integrity first, reps second.

**Operating framework (updated 8/11):** Bhupinder handed off autonomy — within movements not explicitly excluded, symptoms are the gate and the call is yours. This document is a reference, not a permission system.

**What "symptoms" covers.** Pain is the primary gate, but it isn't the only signal that matters here, because two of your findings never presented as pain in the first place:
- **Paresthesia** — tingling, numbness, or burning in the ring/pinky (ulnar) or thumb/dorsal hand (radial) distributions. The 8/11 ulnar finding is paresthesia, not pain.
- **Weakness without pain** — the 7/14 eval found radial-distribution weakness on a maneuver that produced no pain at all.
- **Motor changes** — grip weakness, visible muscle changes, or positional clawing of the ring/pinky. These warrant contacting a provider rather than waiting for Sept 3.

Not a reason to hold back on anything. Just the reason the gate is "symptom-free" rather than strictly "pain-free."

## Section 1 — HEP: Active Program

### Seated Levator Scapulae Stretch
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 1–4, 3–4x/day · **Dose:** 2–5 reps, 10–15 sec hold
**Update (8/11):** Supine is the preferred position now — seated/standing competes with neck stabilization and blunts the stretch. Wall slides stay wall-based; don't conflate the two.
- Pin the shoulder down and back before you tilt — you're stretching the neck, not shrugging into it
- Ear travels toward shoulder in a straight line, no forward head drift
- If fingertips warm up during the hold: back off angle and hold time immediately, log which fingers — that's nerve tension, not a stretch you push through

### Tennis Elbow Self Massage (Desensitization)
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 2, 2–3x/day · **Dose:** 5–10 min per session
- Silicone cupping or manual glide, light pressure — this is a nervous-system dose, not a soft-tissue release
- Direction: circular, both clockwise and counter-clockwise, plus long strokes elbow → wrist along the extensors
- Never hard friction or trigger-point work on this arm — that's an explicit exclusion, not a preference

### Vibration Desensitization
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 4 · **Dose:** interchangeable with Block 2 massage for the shared 2–3x/day dose
- Electric toothbrush along the radial nerve distribution
- Same rule as massage: light, brief exposure, not intensity-chasing

### Seated Wrist Flexion Stretch
**Status:** ✅ (official MedBridge entry) · **Block:** 1–4, 3–4x/day · **Dose:** 10–30 sec hold
**Note:** Your finger-glide variant is a personal technique, not a MedBridge instruction — keep logging this one as the seated version; note the glide as how you personally execute it.
- Use each finger as a guide — "OK sign" progression finger by finger
- Rotate palm as tolerated; do not chase end-range

### Finkelstein Stretch
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 2–4 only (excluded from Block 1 pre-lift — don't pre-load radial tissue before training) · **Dose:** 3–5 reps, progressive hold
- Thumb tucked, fist gently closed, ulnar-deviate the wrist
- Progressive means exactly that — deepen session over session, not rep over rep. Avoid overstretching.

### Prone Chest Stretch on Chair
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 4, 3x/day · **Dose:** 2–3 reps, 10–30 sec, progressive, no aggravation
- Chest open, shoulder blades stay down — don't let the shrug creep in to fake range
- Any pull past "gentle" is past the intended dose

### Scapular Wall Slides
**Status:** ✅ · **Block:** 1x/day · **Dose:** 5 reps (checklist gate on lifting days, not scored)
- Elbows and wrists stay glued to the wall the entire rep — the instant they peel off, the rep stops training the right thing
- Pillowcase between hands/wall reduces friction so you feel the glide, not the grip
- Slow, controlled, zero trunk lean to cheat range

### Standing Bicep Stretch at Wall
**Status:** 💤 DORMANT · Deprioritized 8/11 — "no longer clinically necessary," not an exclusion
- Kept for history only. Palm-up orientation was the corrected form (overriding the printed handout's palm-down) — relevant if this ever gets reactivated.

### Standing Wall Ball Circles (mini Swiss ball, 2–3 lb)
**Status:** ✅ NEW 8/11 · **Block:** 1 (confirmed placement) · **Dose:** 1–3x/day, 1 min, progress hold over time
- Ball stays pinned between hand and wall — the control is in resisting the ball, not chasing bigger circles
- Small, precise circles beat big sloppy ones — this is a proprioceptive drill, not a shoulder-burner

### Full Plank with Scapular Protraction/Retraction AROM
**Status:** ✅ NEW 8/11 · **Block:** 4 · **Dose:** 1x/day, 5 reps, 10–15 sec hold
- Brace the trunk like a plank should be braced — this isn't a scap drill riding on a collapsed core
- Push the floor away (protraction) then let the blades pinch together (retraction) — small range, full control
- If the plank itself is compromising form, drop to a wall or incline version rather than force it

### Standing Subscapularis Lift-Off
**Status:** ✅ NEW 8/11 · **Block:** 4 · **Dose:** 1x/day, 3–5 reps, walk-to-tension hold
- Hand behind the low back, lift away from the spine — small motion, the subscapularis doesn't need range to work
- "Walk to tension" means build into it gradually, not snap to end-range

## Section 2 — Training Pool: Strength Days (Mon/Wed/Fri)

Each entry: **Setup** (what you build before the rep exists) → **Execution** (the rep itself) → **Checkpoints** (what tells you it's right or wrong) → **Injury notes** where the movement touches your current constraints.

Hardstyle principle running through all of it: tension is the lift. You create the position, you don't find it mid-rep. Irradiation — grip and bracing generate whole-body stiffness — matters more than usual for you right now, because the compensations you're working around (right lat under-engagement, scapular instability) are exactly what sloppy tension lets through.

### Deadlift
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Monitor grip-side symptoms · Currently moderate loads

**Setup**
1. Bar over midfoot — laces, not toes. Shins nearly vertical before you bend.
2. Hip hinge down to the bar, knees bending second. Shins meet the bar; don't push the bar forward.
3. Grip: double overhand, just outside the legs. Crush the bar — this is your irradiation source, and on the right side it's also your symptom monitor.
4. **Set the lats before you pull.** Cue: *"protect your armpit."* Chest up, blades pulled down toward back pockets, not pinched together.
5. Big breath into the belly, brace 360° like you're about to be punched. Hold it.
6. **Pull the slack out** — apply upward tension until the bar clicks into the plates. Dead weight off a loose bar is exactly how the right lat gets skipped.

**Execution**
- Push the floor away with the legs while the torso angle stays constant. The bar and hips rise together — hips shooting first means it becomes a back lift.
- Bar drags up the legs, stays in contact or close. Any drift forward and the lat has disengaged.
- Lock out by driving hips through, not by leaning back. Glutes finish it; the spine doesn't hyperextend.
- Descent: hips back first, bar past the knees, then bend the knees. Controlled, not a drop.
- Reset fully between reps — re-brace, re-set lats, re-pull slack. Touch-and-go trains through the exact position where your right side lets go.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Bar path is vertical; ✅ hips and shoulders rise at the same rate; ✅ you can feel both armpits working under load
- ❌ Bar swings out from the shins = lats disengaged
- ❌ Hips shoot up first = you set up too low or lost the brace
- ❌ Right side "goes quiet" on set one = pre-activation didn't take (see below)

**Injury notes**
- **Right lat under-engagement is a firing/coordination gap, not a red flag** — but it's the reason the slack-pull and armpit cue aren't optional here.
- **Pre-activate before working sets:** Band Pull-Aparts or Prone Y-Raises immediately prior. This isn't a warm-up formality — it's the thing that makes set one behave like set three.
- Grip-side symptoms are the monitor. Aching/fatigue in the forearm is expected under load; tingling, shooting, or numbness is a stop.
- Consider unilateral rowing on other days to build the right side without left-side compensation masking it.

### Goblet Squat
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Primary back squat substitute

**Setup**
1. DB or KB held vertically at chest, both hands cupping the top. Elbows tucked under the load, not flared.
2. **Load it kick-up-from-the-knees style** — no clean motion on the right side.
3. Feet shoulder-width or slightly wider, toes out 15–30°. Screw the feet into the floor to build hip external rotation tension.
4. Ribs down, brace before the first inch of movement.

**Execution**
- Break at hips and knees together, sitting down between the feet rather than back like a hinge.
- Elbows drive down inside the knees at depth — this is the whole reason it's your squat sub. The anterior load lets the torso stay upright without the front-rack position that's excluded for you.
- Depth as far as you can hold a neutral spine and keep both heels planted. Don't chase range you can't own.
- Drive up through the whole foot, hips and chest rising together.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Torso stays near-vertical; ✅ knees track over toes; ✅ heels never lift
- ❌ Elbows flaring wide = load creeping into a front-rack-ish position, which defeats the substitution
- ❌ Chest caving forward = brace lost or load too heavy for current position

**Injury notes**
- Elbow flexion under load is present here. Watch for medial elbow contact/pressure against the ribs at depth — direct cubital tunnel contact is a new caution since 8/11.

### Safety Bar Squat
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Back squat substitute

**Setup**
1. Bar centered on the traps, handles in front. Hands on the handles or crossed on the chest — **not** reaching back to grip a straight bar.
2. Unrack with a full brace, walk out in two or three steps, plant.
3. Feet set as with goblet; ribs down, brace.

**Execution**
- Same descent mechanics as a back squat — hips back and down, knees tracking.
- The camber pulls you forward: actively resist with upper back tension, drive the chest up out of the hole.
- Ascend hips-and-chest together; don't let the hips beat the shoulders up.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ You never grip a bar behind your back; ✅ upper back stays engaged against the forward pull
- ❌ Torso folding forward = the camber is winning; reduce load

**Injury notes**
- This is cleared **specifically because** the handle position removes the shoulder external rotation + wrist extension demand that makes a traditional back squat a problem for you. If you ever find yourself reaching back for a bar, you're doing the excluded version.

### Leg Press
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Back squat substitute — zero upper-limb demand

**Setup**
1. Back and hips flush against the pad, no gap in the low back.
2. Feet shoulder-width, mid-platform, whole foot in contact.
3. Hands on the handles lightly — **do not death-grip.** This is the one lower-body movement with no reason to load the right forearm.

**Execution**
- Lower under control until knees reach roughly 90° or the point just before the pelvis tucks off the pad.
- Press through the whole foot. Stop just short of knee lockout — don't slam it out.
- Slow eccentric is where the value is; don't let the sled ride you down.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Low back stays flat against the pad the whole rep; ✅ knees track over toes
- ❌ Butt lifting off the pad at the bottom = too deep for your current range
- ❌ White-knuckle grip on the handles = unnecessary right-arm loading

### Floor Push-Ups
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Renamed 8/12 from "Wall Push-Up Progression" — 5-pain-free-rep goal met
*(Internal tracking key stays "Wall Push-Up Progression" for PR history continuity.)*

**Setup**
1. Hands slightly wider than shoulders, under the shoulder joint, fingers spread. Screw the hands outward into the floor to build shoulder stability.
2. Full plank: glutes and quads on, ribs down, a straight line from ear to ankle.
3. Brace before the descent — this is a moving plank, not an arm exercise.

**Execution**
- Elbows at roughly 45° from the torso — not flared to 90°, not pinned to the ribs.
- Lower under control until chest is a fist's height from the floor (or your current pain-free depth).
- Press away and **protract at the top** — push the floor away so the shoulder blades wrap forward. That protraction is the serratus work, which is the point for you.
- Body moves as one piece. Hips sagging or piking means the plank broke.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Straight line head to heels the whole rep; ✅ full protraction at lockout
- ❌ Hips leading up = trunk brace lost, elbow now absorbing compensatory load
- ❌ Elbows flaring wide = the shoulder position that historically aggravates you

**Injury notes**
- Push-ups appear in the 7/14 eval as a **symptom-aggravating movement**. Reaching the goal doesn't mean the ceiling is gone — depth and volume are still things to progress gradually, not jump.
- Any return of lateral elbow or forearm pain mid-set: stop the set, log it, regress to incline or wall for that session. Don't finish the reps to save the log entry.

### DB Shoulder Press
**Status:** 🆕 REINTRODUCED (8/12, under autonomy) · Was pulled from the pool pending 8/11

**Setup**
1. Seated with back support, or standing if you want the trunk demand. Seated first while you rebuild this pattern.
2. **Kick the DBs up from the knees** — no clean motion on the right side. This doesn't change just because the press is back.
3. Start position: DBs at shoulder height, elbows slightly forward of the torso (not flared into a wide T), wrists stacked directly over elbows.
4. Ribs down, glutes tight, brace. An overhead press with a loose trunk turns into a low-back arch.

**Execution**
- Press up and slightly in, so the DBs finish over the shoulder joint rather than out in front of it.
- Neutral or semi-pronated grip is easier on the right side than fully pronated — the neutral grip reduces the wrist-extension and shoulder-rotation demand.
- Full lockout without shrugging the shoulders into the ears at the top.
- Control the eccentric back to shoulder height. Don't let the DBs drop into the bottom position — that's where the aggravating shoulder angle lives.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Ribs stay down, no low-back arch; ✅ wrists stacked over elbows the whole path; ✅ symmetrical bar path between arms
- ❌ Right arm lagging or taking a different path = the coordination gap showing up overhead; stop the set
- ❌ Elbows flaring into a wide T at the bottom = the position closest to your confirmed aggravators
- ❌ Low back arching to complete the rep = load too heavy for the current position

**Injury notes**
- **This one earns a slower ramp than the rest of the pool.** Chest press is a named aggravator in the 7/14 eval, and overhead pressing shares the shoulder mechanism. That's not a reason to avoid it — it's the reason to start well under what you could do and add load across sessions rather than within one.
- Suggested re-entry: light DBs, seated, partial-to-full range as it feels clean, 2–3 sessions before pushing load.
- Watch the **bottom third** specifically. The 7/14 eval describes chest press aggravating "at a certain point in the motion" — for pressing patterns, that's typically the deep position, not lockout.
- Neutral grip and a slightly narrower elbow path are the two modifications that make this friendlier to the right side. Use them by default; widen only if it stays clean.

### Rope Triceps Pressdown
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · **Explicitly prioritized** per OT guidance — triceps work is on the "do more of this" list

**Setup**
1. Rope on a high pulley. Neutral grip, thumbs on top of the rope ends.
2. Stand tall, slight forward lean at the hips, feet staggered or hip-width.
3. **Elbows pinned to the ribs** — this is the entire setup. If they migrate, the lat and shoulder take over the movement.
4. Ribs down, brace lightly.

**Execution**
- Extend from the elbow only. Upper arm does not move.
- At lockout, spread the rope ends apart and slightly back — that's the peak contraction.
- Full extension without hyperextending or snapping the elbow.
- Control the eccentric back to about 90°; don't let the stack pull the arm up.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Upper arms motionless; ✅ movement happens only below the elbow
- ❌ Shoulder swinging into it or torso rocking = load too heavy, and you've turned a prioritized triceps movement into a compensation drill
- ❌ Wrist extending hard at lockout = watch this; loaded full wrist extension is a caution for you. Keep the wrist neutral.

**Injury notes**
- Neutral wrist throughout. The rope's advantage over a straight bar is exactly that it lets the wrist stay neutral.
- This is the highest-value strengthening movement in your current pool relative to the diagnosis — treat it accordingly, not as an accessory afterthought.

### Farmer's Carry / Suitcase Carry
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Same grip-loading category as deadlift

**Setup**
1. DBs or KBs on the floor beside you. **Deadlift them up** with a hinge — don't bend and snatch them off the ground.
2. Stand tall: ribs stacked over pelvis, blades down and back, chin level.
3. Crush the handles. Grip tension is the point of the exercise, not a byproduct.

**Execution**
- Walk with short, deliberate, controlled steps. No shuffling, no rushing the distance.
- Shoulders stay packed — don't let the load pull them into a shrug-and-forward-round.
- Breathe behind the brace: short breaths, trunk stays stiff.
- Set the weights down under control with a hinge, same as you picked them up.

**Suitcase (single-side) specifics**
- The load is trying to bend you sideways. Resisting that is the whole exercise.
- Stay perfectly upright — no leaning away from the load to counterbalance, no hiking the free-side hip.
- Free arm hangs relaxed at the side. Don't stick it out for balance.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Torso vertical from the front; ✅ shoulders level; ✅ grip holding, not slipping
- ❌ Leaning away from the load = you've made it easier and removed the anti-lateral-flexion work
- ❌ Shoulder creeping toward the ear = blade position lost

**Injury notes**
- Grip-side symptoms are the monitor, same as deadlift.
- **KB racked carry is a worse swap, not an equivalent one** — the rack position puts sustained elbow flexion + medial elbow contact against the forearm, which now runs into the ulnar caution on top of the flexion demand. Straight carries load the grip; racked carries load the elbow.

### Broad Jump
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Superset Block C with Suitcase Carry and Cable Pullover

**Setup**
1. Feet hip-width, athletic stance, on a forgiving surface if available.
2. Arms free — no load, no implements.

**Execution**
- Countermovement: hips back, knees bend, arms swing back together.
- Explode — triple extension through ankle, knee, hip, arms swinging forward and up.
- **Land soft.** Hips back, knees bent, absorb through the whole foot. Landing stiff-legged is how a power drill becomes a joint problem.
- **Full reset between reps.** Stand up, re-set the stance, then jump again. This is a power movement — quality per rep, not reps per minute.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Same distance rep to rep (drop-off means fatigue — stop the set); ✅ quiet, absorbed landings
- ❌ Loud, stiff landings = not absorbing
- ❌ Rushing reps to beat the superset clock = you've turned it into conditioning

**Injury notes**
- Arm swing is unloaded and self-limiting — this is one of the few movements in the pool with essentially no right-arm constraint. Use it as such; don't self-limit the swing unnecessarily.

### Cable Pullover (single-arm)
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED — new, untested, no established load or pace
*Held pending 8/11 due to overlap risk with restrictions; cleared and added to Block C on 8/12.*

**Setup**
1. High pulley, single handle, neutral grip. Face the stack.
2. Step back to create tension at the start position — arm extended overhead-ish, not slack.
3. Hips slightly hinged, ribs down, split or staggered stance for a stable base.
4. Working arm slightly bent and **stays that angle** the entire set. This is not a straight-arm lever.

**Execution**
- Initiate from the **shoulder blade and lat**, not the hand. The blade depresses and the arm follows.
- Pull the handle down and in toward the hip in an arc. Elbow stays soft.
- Stop when the arm reaches the torso — no need to drag it behind you.
- Control the return all the way back to tension. The eccentric is the lat-lengthening portion and it's the part worth having.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Movement originates at the blade; ✅ elbow angle constant; ✅ ribs stay down
- ❌ Torso extending backward to move the weight = load too heavy
- ❌ Elbow bending progressively through the rep = it's become a pressdown
- ❌ Shoulder shrugging up at the start = you've lost the depression that makes this a lat movement

**Injury notes**
- **Start deliberately light.** Week one at any load — the first two or three sessions exist to establish a baseline, not to find a working weight.
- This was flagged for overlap risk with your restrictions before clearance. Overhead-ish arm position with load is the reason. Any lateral elbow, forearm, or nerve-distribution symptom is a stop and a log entry.
- Block C timing (`mins:9, bells:3`) hasn't been adjusted for a third exercise — open training-feel decision after you've actually run it.

### Prone Y-Raises
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED (confirmed by absence-check 8/12) · Also serves as deadlift pre-activation

**Setup**
1. Prone on an incline bench, roughly 30–45°. Chest supported, feet braced.
2. Arms hanging down, thumbs up (external rotation).
3. Bodyweight-arm-only or very light DBs. This is a targeting drill, not a loaded raise.

**Execution**
- Raise the arms to a **30–45° diagonal from the torso** — the "Y." Thumbs lead, pointing up and out.
- Initiate from the lower traps: think blades sliding down and in *before* the arms rise.
- Pause briefly at the top. Lower under full control.
- Small range done correctly beats big range done with the upper traps.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Arm path forms a clear Y; ✅ shoulders stay away from the ears
- ❌ **Arms tracking straight overhead ("I" position)** — this is the exact fault flagged in your 8/9 video review. It reduces lower-trap and serratus targeting, which is the whole reason you're doing them.
- ❌ Shrugging at the top = upper traps have hijacked it; drop the load

**Injury notes**
- Watch bench-edge contact at the medial elbow when set up prone — direct cubital tunnel pressure is a caution since 8/11. Pad it or adjust position if you feel contact.
- As deadlift pre-activation: 1–2 light sets immediately before working sets. Priming, not fatiguing.

### Band Pull-Aparts
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Also serves as deadlift pre-activation

**Setup**
1. Light band, hands shoulder-width or slightly wider, palms down or neutral.
2. Arms extended straight out at chest height. Slight tension in the band at the start.
3. Stand tall, ribs down, glutes lightly on.

**Execution**
- Pull the band apart by driving the shoulder blades together — **arms stay long the whole time.**
- Band travels toward the chest as the hands separate.
- Pause at end-range with the blades squeezed.
- Return slowly under control. The eccentric is where this one actually does its work.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Elbows stay locked-ish; ✅ blades do the moving; ✅ no rib flare
- ❌ Bending the elbows = it's become a row
- ❌ Leaning back or flaring the ribs to get more range = compensation

**Injury notes**
- **This targets rear delts and scapular retraction — it is not a lat-firing drill.** Don't substitute it for Y-raises expecting the same deadlift carryover; they prime different things. Using both before deadlifts is fine and arguably better.
- Keep the band genuinely light. Heavy bands turn this into a movement your right side will find a way around.

### Glute Bridges
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Zero upper-limb demand

**Setup**
1. Supine, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width, heels close enough that fingertips nearly brush them.
2. Ribs down, low back gently flattened toward the floor — posterior pelvic tilt before you lift.
3. Arms relaxed at the sides.

**Execution**
- Drive through the heels, squeeze the glutes, lift the hips until the torso and thighs form a straight line.
- **Stop at the straight line.** Going higher means arching the low back, not extending the hip.
- Hold the squeeze briefly at the top.
- Lower under control; don't just drop.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Glutes doing the work; ✅ neutral spine at lockout
- ❌ Low back arching at the top = hyperextension replacing hip extension
- ❌ Hamstrings cramping = heels too far out, walk the feet closer

### Scapular Wall Isometric (pillowcase)
**Status:** ✅ CLEARED · Non-scored checklist gate before lifting days, 5 reps

**Setup**
1. Face the wall, pillowcase between forearms/hands and the wall.
2. Forearms on the wall, elbows at roughly 90°, shoulder-width.
3. Stand close enough that a slight forward lean loads the arms lightly.

**Execution**
- Press the forearms into the wall and hold. Blades stay down and wrapped around the ribcage.
- No trunk lean to generate leverage. No shrugging.
- Hold the position, breathing normally throughout — this is an isometric, not a breath-hold.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Blades stay depressed; ✅ neutral neck; ✅ no trunk cheat
- ❌ Shoulders creeping toward ears = the position is gone, reset

**Injury notes**
- This is a **gate**, not a scored exercise. It runs before every lifting day for a reason — it sets the scapular position that the rest of the session depends on. Skipping it because it isn't scored defeats the purpose.

## Section 3 — Conditioning

### Zone 2 — Bike / Incline Walk (Tue/Thu)
**Status:** ✅ · **Target:** 25–35 min Z2, ≤5 min Z3, zero Z4–Z5
**Your zones (Whoop, HRR-based — RHR 46, Max 185):** Z2 = 129–143 bpm · Z3 = 143–157 · Z4 = 157+

**Setup**
- Bike: seat height so the knee has a slight bend at the bottom of the stroke. Hands rest lightly on the bars — no gripping, no leaning on the arms.
- Treadmill: incline first, speed second. Walking uphill hits zone at a lower speed and with less impact.
- **Disconnect Whoop from the bike console before ending the session** — pairings persist at the hardware level.

**Execution**
- Steady state, no intervals. Find the effort where you could hold a conversation but wouldn't want to write an essay.
- Adjust resistance or incline to hold zone, not pace or speed. The number on the console is irrelevant; the HR is the whole target.
- If you drift into Z3, reduce output immediately rather than riding it out. Five minutes is the ceiling, not a budget to spend.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ HR sits in 129–143 for the bulk of the session; ✅ zero Z4–Z5 time
- ❌ Creeping into Z3 repeatedly = starting too hard, back off the first ten minutes
- ❌ Gripping the handlebars hard on the bike = unnecessary right-forearm loading on a day that should have none

### Backward Walking Lunges
**Status:** ✅ · Closest sled-drag substitute at Equinox (no sled on-site) · Pairs with Broad Jump in superset

**Setup**
1. Stand tall, feet hip-width, hands on hips or holding light DBs at the sides.
2. Ribs down, brace, eyes forward.

**Execution**
- Step **backward** with one leg, landing on the ball of the foot.
- Lower until the back knee is just above the floor and the front thigh is roughly parallel.
- Front shin stays close to vertical; the front knee shouldn't travel far forward.
- Drive through the **front** heel to return to standing. The front leg does the work — that's what makes this the sled analog.
- Alternate legs or complete one side then switch.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Torso upright; ✅ front heel stays planted; ✅ controlled step back, not a drop
- ❌ Leaning forward = the front leg isn't driving
- ❌ Crashing the back knee = descending too fast

**Injury notes**
- If loading with DBs, it's the same grip-loading category as carries. Bodyweight version has zero arm demand — use that on days you want the legs without the forearm.

### Backward Treadmill Walking (incline)
**Status:** ✅ · Secondary sled substitute

**Setup**
1. Start the belt slow — slower than feels necessary. Straddle the belt before stepping on.
2. Set incline first, hold the rails until you've found the rhythm, then let go.

**Execution**
- Step back onto the balls of the feet, roll to the heel, push through the quad.
- Short steps, upright torso.
- Increase incline before speed.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ Rails released once stable; ✅ steady rhythm, no stumbling
- ❌ Death-gripping the rails throughout = both a safety issue and unnecessary forearm loading. If you can't let go, the belt is too fast.

### Bear Crawls
**Status:** ✅ · Sled substitute · **Use judgment given current constraints**

**Setup**
1. Quadruped: hands under shoulders, knees under hips.
2. Hover the knees an inch off the floor. Flat back, ribs down.

**Execution**
- Opposite hand and foot move together, short steps.
- Hips stay low and level — no rotation, no bobbing.
- Slow and controlled. Speed is not the variable here.

**Checkpoints**
- ✅ A glass of water could sit on your low back; ✅ knees stay hovering
- ❌ Hips swaying side to side = steps too long

**Injury notes**
- This is **loaded weight-bearing through the right hand and extended wrist** — the position that runs closest to your full-wrist-extension caution, and it also has a history of limited wrist flexion on that side from the 2012–13 injury.
- Of the three sled substitutes, this is the one to use least. Backward lunges do the same job with no arm demand. If you use it, keep bouts short and stop on any wrist or forearm symptom.
